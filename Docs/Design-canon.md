# Dad Radar Design Canon

> This document is the single source of truth for the Dad Radar project.
>
> All future design decisions should be measured against this document.
> If a future idea conflicts with anything here, this document takes precedence unless intentionally revised.

---

# Project Vision

Dad Radar is not simply a flight tracking application.

It is a handcrafted digital flight operations console designed to help airline families feel connected while a pilot is away from home.

The experience should feel timeless, intentional, calming, and heirloom quality.

The goal is to answer one simple question:

**"Where's Dad?"**

without requiring anyone to study complicated aviation information.

---

# Design Philosophy

Dad Radar should feel like a piece of airline history brought into the modern world.

Technology should disappear behind beautiful craftsmanship.

Everything should feel:

• Calm

• Purposeful

• Warm

• Understated

• Professional

• Built to last

The experience should resemble an airline operations center from the golden age of aviation rather than a modern technology product.

---

# Emotional Goals

The product should create emotional connection rather than simply display information.

Family members should instantly understand:

• Where Dad is

• What he's doing

• Whether he's flying or on the ground

without effort.

The console should feel reassuring rather than technical.

# User Experience Principles

One glance should answer the important questions.

No clutter.

No unnecessary controls.

No hidden menus.

No information overload.

Every module has one clear purpose.

Every animation should communicate information.

The display should remain beautiful even when no flight is active.

---

# Physical Product First

The software exists to support the physical console.

Every UI decision should assume it will eventually live inside the handcrafted walnut enclosure.

The physical console is the primary product.

The web application is the software that powers it.

---

# Console Layout

The console consists of independent modules.

• Split-Flap Flight Board

• Destination Poster

• Moving Map

• Instrument Panel

• Power / Status Module

Each module has a dedicated purpose and should never become overcrowded.

---

# Motion Language

Movement should be subtle.

Examples include:

• Soft pulsing aircraft location

• Split-flap animations

• Gentle gauge movement

• Smooth fades

Avoid:

• Flashing

• Fast animations

• Gaming effects

• Excessive transitions

---

# Materials

Primary materials:

• Walnut

• Brass

• Leather

• Satin black aluminum

• Matte anti-glare glass

Lighting should be warm.

Nothing should appear glossy or plastic.

---

# Color Palette

Warm black

Brass

Cream

Walnut brown

Muted aviation green

Paper tan

Vintage poster colors

Avoid highly saturated modern colors.

---

# Typography

Typography should feel mechanical and timeless.

Use styles inspired by:

• Split-flap boards

• Aircraft placards

• Airline timetables

• Vintage travel posters

---

# Hardware Philosophy

The console should appear serviceable and modular.

It should resemble professional aviation equipment.

Rear access panels, removable modules, and visible fasteners reinforce this design language.

---

# Future Sound Design

Future hardware versions may include:

• Relay click

• Tube warm-up sound

• Split-flap chatter

• Power switch click

Audio should always be subtle.

---

# Non-Negotiables

Never resemble a generic dashboard.

Never resemble a gaming setup.

Never prioritize technology over craftsmanship.

Never sacrifice warmth for features.

Every decision should reinforce the illusion that Dad Radar is a genuine airline operations console.
